Transcript

Frank: Jack moved, yes. Emily didn't leave the job. She's out at that house five days a week. Has been keeping Edith Caldwell's household going for thirty years.

Ann: Even after the check case? Mrs. Caldwell knew about that when she hired her?

Frank: Everybody knew. Edith knew more than most. Emily's mother and Edith grew up together. Best friends since they were girls. When Emily came out after those eleven months, Edith gave her work. Didn't pretend it hadn't happened. Gave her somewhere to start over.

Ann: The checks were for her first husband's medical bills?

Frank: Hospital bills, yes. Doesn't make forgery legal. Does make a difference when you're trying to understand a person. I covered her sentencing. That woman wasn't celebrating getting away with anything.

Ann: And Jack was the gardener until they moved to Elm Street?

Frank: Until Edith got tired of him drinking on the riding mower. Two years ago. A mower that size and a man that drunk are an insurance conversation nobody wants.

Ann: Does he still do things for the family? Errands, that sort of thing?

Frank: Here and there. There's Emily's boy, too. Took Jack's surname as a youngster. Went to college, came home looking prosperous. Whitlock Wealth Partners on the square. Manages Edith's money. Some talk he's stretched thin, but talk isn't a balance sheet.

Ann: Emily's son. What's his first name?

Frank: James. James Whitlock.
