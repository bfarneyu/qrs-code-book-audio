Ann: Once you hear the chain all three times, it's obviously Jack's truck. We had that connection before we even knew his name.

Ruiz: You had a reason to compare the sightings. A chain sound doesn't identify a driver. The photograph, registration, and Jack's account do work the sound cannot do.

Ann: Same with the throat-clear on the threatening call? Even with the phrase he used at the service entrance?

Ruiz: Those are reasons to ask him questions. His admission confirms he made the call. Don't turn a familiar phrase into a voice identification your training doesn't support.

Ann: And Susan's latch and whistle put her at the Center, not her office. Marcus remembers her there too.

Ruiz: His observation matters. So does his own recording of the confrontation. The bank supplied records we could obtain and check. Those establish the loss independently.

Ann: I want to say we solved it by listening. That leaves out a lot, doesn't it?

Ruiz: Listening gave you questions. Records, witnesses, and lawful recordings let us test the answers. Preserve the originals. Keep your observations separate from your conclusions. And don't call an illegal interception courtroom proof.
