Transcript

Ned: This reads like somebody selling a tractor. If you want a personal sentence, give me one she might actually use. I can't get that from a signature sample.

James: Keep it short. The important part is the disposition, not whether anyone is moved by the sentiment. We don't need a whole family history.

Susan: Use my Susie-Q in the personal bit. Put it after to, then use my full name where it has to be legal. That's something she'd say.

Ned: You're supplying that wording. I'm not putting in something you come back next week and tell me sounds wrong. Are you sure you want it?

Susan: Yes. I know my own grandmother. She doesn't sound like a bank brochure when she's talking about me. Leave the rest plain if you're worried.

James: And the date is May tenth. Later than the April paper, earlier than placement. Do not confuse it with the June transfer date.

Ned: I keep separate files. You keep the dates straight on your end. Nobody gets a finished page until somebody has checked every name and every number.

Susan: Send the wording to me first. He'll check the figures. I'll check whether it sounds like a person who lives in that house.
