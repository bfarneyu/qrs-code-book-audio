Transcript

Ned: Before anybody opens that envelope, tell me which thing you're buying. You called it a correction on the phone. Corrections don't usually need a meeting behind a closed mill.

James: We need something that will stand up to ordinary inspection. Nobody is asking you to defend it in front of a judge.

Ned: Everybody says ordinary inspection until somebody ordinary takes a second look. For the will, I need clean samples, not a photograph somebody took sideways through glass.

Susan: There are letters in there. Different years, different pens. The recent ones are clipped together. Don't copy the way she wrote twenty years ago.

Ned: I'll choose what I can use. I also need to know who touches the household copy, where it stays, and whether somebody counts the pages.

James: The man at the road can answer household questions. You deal with me about the work. He does not need to hear your price.

Susan: He isn't coming over here. He said that before we left. If you need him, arrange it somewhere else and let us get out of sight.

Ned: Fine. But nobody leaves until we've finished the terms. Your envelope isn't an agreement, and I haven't accepted your money yet.
