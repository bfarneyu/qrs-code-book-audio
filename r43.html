Transcript

Ann: Her lawyer says she only panicked about the will in May. That she didn't know James had been stealing before then.

Paul: Set the lawyer's statement beside the search return. Not beside your opinion of Susan. What did Edith keep?

Ann: The envelopes and the simplified monthly summaries. Twelve months of them in her desk. Susan brought those over from James's office.

Paul: That was where they were said to come from. The printer evidence tells us something more useful. Which machine produced the pages?

Ann: The Hewlett-Packard on Susan's carriage-house desk. The paper and printer markings match. It's not just her being in the room while James explains taxes.

Paul: And the dates run back before the forged will. That's the distinction. A later forgery doesn't by itself prove she knew about a year of false statements.

Ann: But she said she'd sent them. I heard her correct herself and still let it go past me. I was waiting for the house plans.

Paul: Now we have paper to check against the words. Write that. Don't make the pronoun carry the whole case.
